Moving into a new home: what to do before the furniture arrives

Between handover and moving day, the home stands empty. That is the best time you will ever get to renovate. Once the sofa, the beds and the boxes are in, everything gets slower and dustier. Here is a simple order of work that saves you doing things twice.

In short
  • Book tradespeople as soon as you know the handover date. Many have waiting lists.
  • Work from the top down: electrician and ceiling first, then walls, then floors, and a deep clean last.
  • Do the dusty jobs that need empty rooms before you move in.
  • On handover day, read the electricity meter, test the smoke alarms and consider changing the lock.

On handover day

  • Read the electricity meter and tell your electricity supplier you have taken over.
  • Test the smoke alarms and check there is a fire extinguisher.
  • Consider changing the lock. You do not know how many keys exist.
  • Photograph every room. It helps if you find faults later and when you plan the renovation.
  • Find the main water valve and the fuse box.

Book tradespeople early

The most common mistake is waiting until you have the keys. Many tradespeople in Norway have waiting lists, especially before Christmas and in spring. Call around as soon as you know the handover date, get site visits and quotes, and make a plan. Some things are made to measure and must be ordered in advance, such as kitchens, wardrobes and stretch ceilings.

The right order: top down

  1. Electrician runs new cables, sockets and points for ceiling lights. This must happen before ceilings and walls are closed.
  2. Ceiling. Filling and painting, new boards or a stretch ceiling.
  3. Walls. Paint and wallpaper.
  4. Floors come last so they are not damaged by ladders and paint.
  5. Cleaning. A deep clean after the tradespeople, before the removal van arrives.

Do the dusty work while the rooms are empty

Sanding filler and floors creates fine dust that settles everywhere. In an empty room that is a problem for a few hours. In a furnished room it is a problem for weeks.

The ceiling is easy to forget

People think about floors, kitchens and paint, and notice the ceiling only when they lie in bed and look up. Old ceiling boards, cracks, stains and a single cable hanging in the middle are common in older homes. The ceiling is also the hardest thing to deal with once you have moved in.

A stretch ceiling is installed below the old one. There is no filling, no sanding and hardly any dust, and a normal room is usually finished in one day. Spotlights and light lines can be fitted at the same time, and cables are hidden above the membrane. The membrane is made to measure, so get in touch as soon as you know your handover date.

Do not do everything at once

It is tempting to fix everything before moving in. Often it is wiser to live in the home for a while before deciding on the kitchen and bathroom. Use the time before moving in for the jobs that make dust and need empty rooms.

Common questions

What should I renovate before moving in?

The jobs that create dust or need empty rooms: electrical work, ceilings, painting and floors. Kitchens and bathrooms can often wait until you have lived there a while.

In what order should I renovate?

From the top down: electrician first, then ceilings, then walls and finally floors. Finish with a deep clean before the furniture arrives.

How long does a new ceiling take?

A stretch ceiling in a normal room is usually installed in one day. The membrane is made to measure, so it has to be ordered in advance.
